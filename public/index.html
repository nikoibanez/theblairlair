THE RATON ACCOUNT

Field notes from the Blair problem, filed somewhere between vermin, mildew damage, an unpaid tab, and a very poor night's sleep.

I began asking about Blair because too many people in Raton had the same nervous little pause before saying they had never heard of him, which is the kind of pause that gets a notebook opened. By the third conversation I had acquired two contradictory maps, a photograph of somebody's drainpipe, a motel napkin marked with a circle around nowhere useful, and firm advice from a man outside a bar that I should stop looking upward in alleys.

The witnesses disagree about everything useful, yet the useless details arrive with pathological precision: wet concrete underfoot, yellow eyes where no eyes belonged, a long tail moving behind something built like an exhausted adult man, and the sour tobacco smell of a coat that had slept outdoors longer than its owner. Nobody can agree whether Blair walks, crawls, or simply arrives halfway through whatever bad decision is already underway.

His territory appears to follow appetite instead of geography, drifting toward railroad edges, dumpsters behind respectable buildings, unlocked gates, roofs with poor drainage, and any parking lot where a person has just said, "This will probably be fine." The pattern is less predation than participation; Blair seems to want the evening to continue until everyone involved has made one choice too many.

At 2:13 one morning I heard scratching above an alley awning and looked up long enough to see a shape withdraw with the guilty speed of something that understood shame but had chosen against it. I left Raton with no proof worth keeping anywhere except a damp shoebox and a standing recommendation that if Blair asks where you are going, tell him Colorado.

"The animal, if animal is the word, has the manners of a trespasser and the timing of a landlord."

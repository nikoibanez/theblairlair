THE RATON ACCOUNT

Nobody agrees when Blair first started showing up.

Some put him in the alleys behind downtown. Others put him along the railroad cut after midnight. The details stay embarrassingly consistent: a man’s silhouette, a rat’s face, bright yellow eyes, and the smell of wet concrete and somebody else’s cigarette.

Blair does not arrive for tragedy. He arrives after petty crimes, bad hookups, missing beer, busted locks, stolen extension cords, inexplicable stains, and decisions nobody wants to explain in daylight.

Sometimes you hear him in the walls. Sometimes in the alley. Sometimes in your own head. Once you hear the call, the local advice is simple: do not answer correctly.

“If Blair asks where you’re headed, lie.”
